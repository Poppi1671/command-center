### Lesson 1.4: The R.C.G.F. Formula: Making AI Produce Client-Ready Work

*Video script (about 11 min)*

Here's why most people get junk from AI: they type one line and hope. Professionals give the machine four things every time. **Role. Context. Goal. Format.** R.C.G.F.

**Role:** who the AI should be. "You are a real estate copywriter with 15 years of experience in Central Florida."

**Context:** what it needs to know. "The home is a 3-bed, 2-bath, 1,650 square feet, built 2004, new roof 2024, screened lanai, in Davenport near Posner Park. Buyers are young families and retirees."

**Goal:** the exact job. "Write a listing description that makes a buyer want to schedule a showing."

**Format:** what it looks like. "150 to 180 words. Two short paragraphs. No exclamation points. End with a one-line call to action. Give me three versions with different openings."

Watch what happens when we run that. [Screen demo: paste the four-part prompt, read the three outputs.] That's client-ready in ninety seconds. Now the professional step: **you edit.** Fix one fact, cut one adjective, add one local detail the AI couldn't know. Your edit is what the client pays for. AI drafts, you finish.

Two more moves. **Ask for options:** "give me three versions" always. Clients love choosing. **Ask for a critique:** paste your edited version back and say "You are a picky buyer. What would make you skip this listing?" Fix what it finds.

Your assignment: write one R.C.G.F. prompt for your lane. Run it. Edit the output. Save the prompt in your toolkit under a name like "Listing, standard." That's prompt number one of ten.

**Key points**
- Role, Context, Goal, Format: every prompt, every time.
- Always ask for three versions.
- AI drafts, you finish. Your edit is the product.

**Worksheet 1.4:** R.C.G.F. builder: four boxes to fill, one for the critique prompt, one for the saved prompt name.

**Quiz 1.4**

### Lesson 2.1: Why Ten Prompts Beat a Thousand

*Video script (about 8 min)*

Last week you learned R.C.G.F. and saved one prompt. This week you build ten, and here's why ten is the number. A professional doesn't improvise every job. A plumber doesn't invent a new wrench for every sink. You will have ten tools, each one tested, each one named, and you'll reach for the right one without thinking.

The ten break into three groups.

**Drafting prompts (four):** the ones that produce the deliverable: the listing, the caption set, the newsletter, the blog post.

**Fixing prompts (three):** the ones that improve what you've got: the critique prompt, the shorten-and-sharpen prompt, the "match this voice" prompt.

**Business prompts (three):** the ones that run your side business: the outreach message writer, the proposal writer, the client-question answerer.

Every prompt lives in your toolkit doc with a name, the R.C.G.F. text, and one line about when to use it. When you land a client, you don't start from a blank screen. You open the doc, pick the prompt, change three words, run it.

One rule for the week: **a prompt isn't in the toolkit until you've run it three times on three different real inputs and liked all three.** Testing is the work. Writing the prompt takes two minutes.

Your assignment: on the worksheet, write the names of your ten prompts for your lane. Just the names. "Listing, standard. Listing, luxury. Captions, 20 pack. Review reply, happy. Review reply, angry…" We fill them in through the week.

### Lesson 2.2: The Four Drafting Prompts (with the exact text)

*Video script (about 12 min)*

"You are a [senior copywriter for real estate agents / church communications director / social media manager for local barbershops] with 15 years of experience. **Prompt D2, The Premium Version**
Same as D1, add: "Make this version feel premium and calm. No hype words. No exclamation points. Use specific sensory details and one unexpected observation."

**Prompt D3, The Batch**
"Using the facts below, produce [30 days of captions / 4 weekly emails / 10 FAQ answers] in one go. Vary the opening of each. Mark each one with a theme tag: [tip, story, offer, proof, question]. Output as a table: #, theme, text."
This is your monthly-retainer prompt. One run, a month of content, you edit for an hour, you invoice.

**Prompt D4, The Rewrite**
"Here is an existing [listing / newsletter / post]. Rewrite it to be clearer, warmer and shorter, keeping every fact. Then list the five changes you made and why."
This is your sample-maker and your door-opener: you can rewrite anyone's existing work and show them the before and after.

Demo: [screen] run D3 for the barber shop: thirty captions in one table. Pick the best twenty. Edit five. That's a $250 deliverable in forty minutes.

### Lesson 2.3: The Three Fixing Prompts: Where the Quality Comes From

*Video script (about 9 min)*

Drafting gets you 80%. Fixing gets you paid. Three prompts.

**Prompt F1, The Picky Reader**
"You are [the pickiest buyer in this price range / a church member who skims / a 24-year-old scrolling fast]. Read this. Tell me the three places you'd stop reading, lose trust, or get bored, and exactly what to change."
Run it on every deliverable before it ships. Fix what it finds. This single habit is the difference between $50 work and $150 work.

**Prompt F2, Shorten and Sharpen**
"Cut this by 30% without losing any fact. Replace every weak verb. Remove every adjective that isn't doing a job. Keep my voice."
Clients almost always want shorter. Give it to them before they ask.

**Prompt F3, Match the Voice**
"Here are three samples of how [client name] writes. Study the rhythm, the words they use, how formal they are. Now rewrite my draft so it sounds like them, not like me."
This is how you keep a client for a year. The barber's captions sound like the barber. The pastor's newsletter sounds like the pastor. Nobody can tell AI was in the room.

Safety reminder from Week 1: when you paste client samples, strip out phone numbers, addresses and anything private first.

Your assignment: run F1 then F2 then F3 on one of your Week 1 samples. Compare the before and the after on the worksheet. Save all three prompts.

**Key points:** F1 Picky Reader on every job · F2 shorter before they ask · F3 voice-match keeps clients for a year.

**Worksheet 2.3:** Before/after box · what F1 caught · word count before/after F2 · three voice samples used for F3.

### Lesson 3.1: Productize: Turn "I can help" into a Thing with a Name

*Video script (about 9 min)*

"I can help with your social media" is not an offer. Nobody can say yes to it, because nobody knows what they're buying. "The 20-Post Monthly Caption Pack, $250, delivered by the 1st" is an offer. It has a name, a quantity, a price, and a date. A busy person can say yes in four seconds.

This is called productizing. You take a service and box it like a product on a shelf. Here's the formula: **Name + What's in the box (three things) + When they get it + What it costs.**

Examples in the four lanes:
- *The Listing Launch Kit:* listing description, 3 social captions, 1 email to the agent's list. 24-hour turnaround. $125 per listing.
- *The Sunday Bulletin Service:* weekly bulletin, monthly newsletter, event announcements as needed. Delivered Thursdays. $175 per month.
- *The Shop Caption Pack:* 20 captions, 5 Google review replies, 1 monthly promo post. Delivered by the 1st. $250 per month.
- *The Practice Content Plan:* 2 blog posts, 4 LinkedIn posts, 1 client FAQ update. Delivered the 15th. $400 per month.

Notice three things. Every offer has exactly **three deliverables**: three feels complete, five feels like homework. Every offer has a **day**, not "soon." Every price is a **round number**, no $247.99.

Give your offer a name that says the result. Not "Robert's Writing Service." *The Listing Launch Kit.* People repeat names like that to their friends.

Your assignment: fill the offer formula on the worksheet. Name, three deliverables, delivery day, price (we test the price in 3.2). Say the whole thing out loud in one breath. If you run out of breath, it's too long.

**Key points:** Productize: name + 3 deliverables + day + price · Three deliverables, a real day, a round number · Name says the result.

### Lesson 3.2: Pricing Without Fear: The Three-Number Method

*Video script (about 11 min)*

Beginners price from fear. They think "what would I pay?" Wrong question. You're not the customer. Here's the method that removes the fear. Three numbers.

**Number one: what they pay now.** Every one of your customers already spends money or hours on this. A realtor pays a copywriter $75–$150 or spends two hours they bill at $100. A church pays a part-timer or burns the pastor's Saturday. Find that number. Ask them: "What do you do for this now?" Your price must be at or below what they pay now, in money or in hours. That's the ceiling.

**Number two: your floor.** How long does the job take you with the toolkit? Thirty minutes to an hour. What do you need per hour to make this worth it? If $50 an hour makes you happy, a one-hour job has a floor of $50. Never go under your floor. Ever.

**Number three: the value.** What's the job worth to them? A listing that sells a week faster. A newsletter that puts 20 more people in seats. A caption pack that books 10 more chairs. If the value is in the thousands and your floor is $50, you have a lot of room. Price in the middle: above your floor, below their ceiling, closer to the ceiling than you're comfortable with.

Real example: listing description. Their ceiling: $125 (that's what the last copywriter charged). Your floor: $50. Value: a listing that shows well sells faster, which is worth thousands. Price: $100. Say it like it's normal. Because it is.

Two rules. **Monthly beats per-job.** Turn $100 listings into "up to 4 listings a month for $350." Predictable for them, predictable for you. **Raise after three yeses.** When three people say yes without flinching, you're too cheap. Add 25% for the next one.

Pause the video and run the three numbers for your offer on the worksheet. Then write the price in pen.

**Key points:** Ceiling (what they pay now), floor (your hourly need), value (what it's worth) · Price between floor and ceiling, closer to ceiling · Monthly beats per-job; raise after three easy yeses.

**Worksheet 3.2:** Ceiling $ · Floor $ · Value $ · My price $ · Monthly version $.

**Quiz 3.2:** 1) The ceiling is: (a) what they pay now ✔ (b) what you'd pay (c) $1,000. 2) Never go under: (a) the ceiling (b) your floor ✔ (c) $10. 3) After three easy yeses: (a) lower price (b) raise 25% ✔ (c) stop selling. 4) Better for both sides: (a) monthly retainer ✔ (b) per-job only.

### Lesson 3.3: The Guarantee and the Boundaries

*Video script (about 8 min)*

Two things turn a maybe into a yes: a guarantee that removes their risk, and boundaries that remove yours.

**The guarantee.** "If the first delivery isn't what you expected, I'll redo it once, free, or refund it. Your call." That's it. You will almost never pay it. But the realtor who's been burned by a flaky freelancer hears it and relaxes. Put it on every offer.

**The boundaries,** written on the offer so you never have the awkward conversation later:
1. *Revisions:* "One round of revisions included. Extra rounds $25."
2. *Turnaround:* "24–48 hours after I receive your facts." Their delay doesn't become your emergency.
3. *What I need from you:* the three things they must send (photos, facts, dates). No facts, no clock.
4. *Payment:* "Half to start, half on delivery" for one-offs. "Billed on the 1st" for monthly. Stripe link. Card on file.
5. *Scope:* "This covers X. Anything else is a quick quote." That's B3 from Week 2, in writing.

Say these with a smile. Professionals have terms. Amateurs have hurt feelings.

Case study: Angela, 57, nurse in Winter Haven, writes patient-education handouts for two clinics. First month she did six "quick little changes" free and resented all six. Month two she added the revision line and the scope line. Clinic one said "fair enough." Clinic two paid $25 twice. Nobody left. Resentment gone.

Your assignment: add the guarantee and the five boundaries to your offer on the worksheet, in your words.

**Key points:** Guarantee: redo once or refund removes their risk · Five boundaries: revisions, turnaround, what I need, payment, scope · Terms in writing, said with a smile.

## MODULE 5: Deliver So Good They Renew: systems, quality, and proof

**Outcome by Friday:** one client delivered on time with a repeatable workflow, a written SOP for your service, and a testimonial in your client's own words.

### Lesson 5.1: The Delivery Workflow: Brief, Build, Check, Send

*Video script (about 9 min)*

Delivery is where new AI businesses leak money. You get a client, you open a chat window, you start typing, and two hours later you've made something that's almost right. The fix is a workflow, the same four steps every time.

**1. Brief.** Turn the client's intake into a one-page brief: who it's for, the goal, the three facts you must include, the tone, the due date. No brief, no build.

**2. Build.** Use your prompt pack from the toolkit. Feed the brief in. Generate a draft. Time-box it: 45 minutes for a bulletin, 30 for a caption pack. If you're past the box, cut scope, not quality.

**3. Check.** Run a checklist before a human sees it: facts match the brief, names spelled right, no invented claims, tone matches, nothing that would embarrass the client. AI makes plausible errors. Your job is to catch them.

**4. Send.** Send on or before the due date, with one line: "Here's your [deliverable]. Two notes: [note]. Reply with changes by [time]."

The workflow is the product. A client doesn't pay for the AI. They pay for a result that arrives on time, every time, without them thinking about it.

**Key points:** Brief → build → check → send · Time-box each build · AI makes plausible errors; the check catches them · Deliver on or before the due date.

**Worksheet 5.1:** Brief template (5 fields) · check list (8 items) · time box for your service.

### Lesson 5.2: Quality Control: The Five-Point Check

*Video script (about 10 min)*

Every deliverable gets the same five checks before it leaves your desk. Make this a habit and you'll never send an embarrassing error.

1. **Facts.** Every name, number, date and claim traces back to the client's brief or a source they gave you. If you can't trace it, remove it.
2. **Names and details.** Spelling of names, addresses, church or company names, product names. Read them aloud once.
3. **Tone.** Does it sound like the client, not like AI? Swap any phrase you wouldn't say.
4. **Length and format.** Matches what was promised in the offer sheet. Same number of items, same structure.
5. **The embarrassment test.** Would the client be embarrassed if their own boss or congregation read this word for word? If yes, fix it.

**The rule for mistakes:** if you find an error after sending, fix it, send the corrected version within the hour, and say one line: "Caught a typo on my end. Corrected version attached." Clients forgive mistakes they see you fix fast. They don't forgive mistakes they find themselves.

Case study (illustrative): Danielle, 44, a Winter Haven real-estate assistant who writes listing descriptions. Her five-point check caught a wrong square footage on a listing before it went live, a number the AI had estimated. She fixed it in ten minutes. The agent called it the best service he'd had.

**Key points:** Five checks: facts, names, tone, format, embarrassment · Fix errors fast, send corrections within the hour · Never invent a number.

**Worksheet 5.2:** Five-point checklist, filled out for one real deliverable.

**Quiz 5.2:** 1) The first check is: (a) tone (b) facts ✔ (c) length. 2) If you can't trace a claim: (a) keep it (b) remove it ✔ (c) guess. 3) After a mistake, send a fix within: (a) a week (b) the hour ✔ (c) never.

### Lesson 6.1: The Revenue Ladder

*Video script (about 9 min)*

Every AI service business has the same shape: a ladder. You don't jump to the top. You climb one rung at a time, and each rung pays for the next.

**Rung 1, Free.** A sample, a test job, a free one-page assessment. This is how strangers become prospects.
**Rung 2, Low-ticket.** $25 to $75. A one-time deliverable like one listing description, one caption pack, one prompt pack. Fast yes, low risk.

**Rung 5, Recurring.** A retainer or membership. The stable base. This is where the business becomes predictable.

Your ladder needs at least Rungs 1, 3 and 5 to work. Rungs 2 and 4 are optional but help cash flow and average order value.

**Key points:** Five rungs: free, low-ticket, core, upsell, recurring · Minimum: rungs 1, 3 and 5 · Each rung pays for the next.

### Lesson 6.2: The Capacity Math

*Video script (about 10 min)*

Before you sell one more client, do the math on how many you can actually serve. This is the step most people skip, and it's why they burn out at client number six.

**Your hours per client per month:** from your Module 5 time tracking. Say a bulletin takes 90 minutes of your time, plus 30 minutes of communication. That's 2 hours.

**Your weekly available hours for delivery:** be honest. If you have 10 hours a week, and you protect 4 hours for outreach and admin, you have 6 for delivery.

**Your maximum clients:** 6 hours per week divided by 2 hours per client per week, if the work is weekly. Or per month, if monthly. Know this number before you sell.

Rule: never sell beyond 80 percent of your capacity. The last 20 percent is for problems, sick days and the client who calls at 9 p.m.

**Worked example:** Marcus delivers a weekly bulletin at about 90 minutes plus 30 minutes of communication, about 2 hours a week per church. He has 6 hours a week for delivery. Maximum: 3 clients. Safe sales target at 80 percent: 2 clients plus one spare slot.

**Key points:** Hours per client, times weekly available hours · Never sell past 80 percent of capacity · Know the max before you sell.

**Worksheet 6.2:** Hours per client · weekly delivery hours · max clients · 80 percent target.

**Quiz 6.2:** 1) Sell up to: (a) 100 percent of capacity (b) 80 percent ✔ (c) 150 percent. 2) Capacity equals: (a) hours you wish you had (b) delivery hours divided by hours per client ✔ (c) your number of followers. 3) The last 20 percent is for: (a) more sales (b) problems and surprises ✔ (c) vacation only.

### Lesson 6.4: Systems That Let You Step Back

*Video script (about 9 min)*

Scale means you do less, and the business does more. Three systems make that possible.

**System 1: Templates.** Your SOP from Module 5 becomes a folder: intake form, brief, prompt pack, check list, delivery message. New client, same folder, faster delivery.

**System 2: Automation.** Connect your intake form to your tracker. When a client signs, a welcome message and the Stripe invoice send automatically. Start with one automation. Add the next only when the first runs without you.
